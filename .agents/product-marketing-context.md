# Product Marketing Context

Last updated: 2026-05-22

## Product Overview

One-liner:

Lionheart is the operations command center for K-12 schools.

What it does:

Lionheart connects the daily work of calendars, events, facilities, IT, forms, approvals, messaging, reporting, and AI into one shared operating view. It helps school staff see what is happening, what is blocked, who owns it, and what needs attention next.

Product category:

K-12 school operations platform.

Product type:

Multi-tenant SaaS.

Business model:

Annual subscription with core plans and add-ons.

## Target Audience

Target companies:

K-12 schools, private school networks, charter schools, and small to mid-size districts.

Decision-makers:

IT directors, heads of school, principals, operations leaders, business managers, and district administrators.

Primary use case:

Replace disconnected tools and spreadsheets used to run non-classroom school operations.

Jobs to be done:

- Know what is happening today across the school.
- Catch conflicts and blockers before they become problems.
- Move requests, approvals, and follow-up work through the right teams.
- Keep facilities, IT, events, forms, and communication connected.
- Produce cleaner reporting and audit evidence.

## Problems And Pain Points

Core problem:

Schools run daily operations through calendars, inboxes, spreadsheets, ticket tools, forms, and one-off systems that do not talk to each other.

Why alternatives fall short:

- Point tools solve one department's problem but create handoff gaps.
- Generic ticketing systems do not understand schools, rooms, calendars, events, campuses, or student/device context.
- Spreadsheets work until accountability, reporting, or multi-school coordination matters.
- AI tools are usually disconnected from permissions, audit trails, and real operational data.

What it costs them:

Time, missed handoffs, duplicate entry, messy reporting, frustrated staff, parent-facing mistakes, and unnecessary vendor sprawl.

Emotional tension:

School leaders feel like they are always reacting. They want calm control before the day gets away from them.

## Differentiation

Key differentiators:

- One school operations graph for rooms, users, teams, schools, calendars, tickets, events, forms, devices, and messages.
- Leo AI can work across modules while respecting permissions and risk levels.
- Built around K-12 operational jobs, not generic project management.
- Strong public/private split for parents, students, substitutes, and event registrants.
- Multi-school context from the start.

How we do it differently:

Lionheart is not a bundle of unrelated modules. The value comes from the connections between them.

Why that is better:

A room conflict, missing approval, open work order, public registration issue, or device blocker can show up in the same daily operating view.

## Objections

| Objection | Response |
| --- | --- |
| "We already use Google Calendar and email." | Lionheart does not replace every communication tool. It connects the operational work those tools cannot coordinate. |
| "This looks like a lot." | The buyer should see one daily command center first, then department-specific depth as needed. |
| "We do not want another system." | Lionheart should replace several disconnected systems, not add another layer. |
| "Is the AI safe?" | Leo is permission-aware, risk-tiered, and designed for confirmable operational actions. |

Anti-persona:

Schools looking for an SIS, LMS, HR/payroll, accounting platform, or public website builder.

## Switching Dynamics

Push:

Too many disconnected tools, manual coordination, missed handoffs, weak reporting, and spreadsheet dependency.

Pull:

One operating view, fewer systems, cleaner accountability, better parent/public workflows, and AI that sees the school context.

Habit:

Staff already know their current mix of email, Google Calendar, spreadsheets, ticketing, and paper forms.

Anxiety:

Migration effort, staff adoption, data security, permissions, and whether one platform can really handle enough of the work.
